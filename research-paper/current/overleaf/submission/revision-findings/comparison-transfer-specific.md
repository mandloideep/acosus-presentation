# Transfer-specific comparison

Revision ID: `REV-COMP-001`.

This is the active comparison approach.

## Selection rationale

The reviewer asked for comparison with existing transfer-student advising systems.

ASSIST and the Transferology/TES product family are directly focused on transfer planning, articulation, and credit evaluation.

They therefore provide a more precise comparison than broad student-success platforms or research prototypes alone.

## Source assessment

ASSIST is documented by its operator as the official repository of articulation and transferability information for California public institutions and participating independent institutions.

Source: https://resource.assist.org/

Nguyen, Doroudi, and Epstein document that students use articulation websites for transfer planning and identify manual comparison and advising needs.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1080/10668926.2024.2356330

CollegeSource documents TES as a staff-facing system for researching transfer credit, routing evaluations, managing equivalencies, and publishing results.

Source: https://collegesource.com/transfer-tools/tes/

CollegeSource documents Transferology as a student-facing tool for checking how courses, standardized exams, and military credit may transfer to participating institutions.

Source: https://transferology-support.collegesource.com/article/51-how-to-use-will-my-courses-transfer

The commercial pages are authoritative for documented features but are not independent evidence of effectiveness.

The comparison therefore describes product scope and avoids claiming undocumented defects.

## Active comparison

| System | Documented purpose and strengths | Scope boundary or weakness relative to ACOSUS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ASSIST | Official articulation and transferability information for California institutions, including major, general education, and elective requirements. | Geographic and articulation focus; cross-agreement comparison and term planning can remain manual, and the documented scope does not center nonacademic transfer context. |
| Transferology and TES | Broad course data, student-facing transfer matching, staff evaluation workflows, and equivalency management. | The documented feature set centers course and credit transfer; absence of ACOSUS-style transfer-experience surveys is treated as a scope difference, not a verified defect. |
| ACOSUS | Structured collection of academic and nonacademic transfer context, linked surveys, readiness scoring, and advisor-visible profiles. | No articulation engine, no validated prediction of longitudinal outcomes, and no formal advisor-dashboard evaluation. |

## Draft interpretation

The systems are complementary rather than interchangeable.

ASSIST and Transferology/TES are substantially stronger for authoritative articulation and credit-equivalency work.

ACOSUS instead explores structured capture of transfer-specific academic and nonacademic context that can be reviewed by advisors and later studied as predictive information.

Its present evidence supports design feasibility and structured data collection, not superiority or predictive validity.
